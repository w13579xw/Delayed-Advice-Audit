Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, ``Auditing the Value of Delayed Advice in Autonomous Agents,'' as a Regular Paper in \emph{Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems}.

The manuscript addresses how to interpret the reward gains of an advice-taking agent. A policy trained with packet features can outperform a separately fitted local-input policy while its deployed decisions and actions do not depend on the returned packet. We document such a case in prospective visual manipulation experiments and give an executable null-policy certificate explaining its behavior.

The contribution is an evaluation framework linking pipeline comparisons, runtime message dependence and measured outcomes under explicitly specified message channels. It combines executable local references, fixed-packet counterfactual branches, decisions recorded before outcomes and native replay audits. A fixed-policy bridge now evaluates the original visual PickPlace system on 60 nonzero-delay environments, alongside 180 state-task environments. Refresh changes actual behavior while positive refresh benefit remains unresolved. An independent generation-time decoder control improves a predefined fresh chunk relative to stale-offset decoding, while the frozen selector does not establish a corresponding benefit. This changes the receiver and is kept separate from the channel study. Finite-pool headroom diagnostics separate available opportunity from harmful selection. A complete visual replication includes three independently trained 15k-budget systems, all local heads and separate arrival critics; full-policy benefit remains unresolved. Supporting condition, tolerance and historical results are retained in a separate Online Resource, keeping the main article focused.

This work fits the journal's interest in agent decision-making architectures and their evaluation. It complements advice acquisition and reuse research by asking which role the newly returned message plays in an already fitted architecture. It does not claim a new best-performing controller or priority for standard counterfactual branching. The evidence is limited to simulated robotic agents; real-robot performance and asynchronous deployment remain untested.
